# Can Machines Think?

## 1956

### **Logic Theorist: The First Artificial Intelligence Program**

In 1956, Allen Newell, Herbert A. Simon, and Clifford Shaw completed the Logic Theorist, a computer program widely considered to be the first artificial intelligence system. Presented at the landmark Dartmouth Workshop, the program was designed to emulate human problem-solving skills by using symbolic reasoning and heuristic search. It successfully proved 38 of the first 52 mathematical theorems in Chapter 2 of Alfred North Whitehead and Bertrand Russell’s *Principia Mathematica*, even discovering a shorter, more elegant proof for Theorem 2.01 than the one originally published by the authors.

* **Logic Theorist:** The first automated theorem-proving program, designed to perform symbolic reasoning and solve problems in formal logic.
* **Heuristic Search:** A problem-solving strategy that uses rules of thumb, educated guesses, or practical shortcuts to find optimal solutions efficiently within a large search space.
* **Dartmouth Workshop (1956):** A seminal summer conference organized by John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester, and Claude Shannon, officially founding artificial intelligence as an academic discipline.
* ***Principia Mathematica*:** A three-volume work on the foundations of mathematics published by Alfred North Whitehead and Bertrand Russell between 1910 and 1913, attempting to derive all mathematical truths from a set of logical axioms.
